## Quality
- [x] `QMPClient` left socket-level failures unwrapped - a QMP response that took longer than the hardcoded 15s read timeout (a hung/overloaded QEMU, a long-running command) raised a raw `TimeoutError` out of `_read_msg`, and a dead connection on `sendall` raised a raw `OSError`/`BrokenPipeError` out of `command`, instead of the module's own `QMPError` every other failure path already uses (bad handshake, closed-by-peer, connect failure). Verified: `ruff check src tests`, `mypy --strict src`, `python -m compileall src`, and all 221 tests pass (8 new `tests/test_qmp.py` cases: timeout during a command wrapped as `QMPError`, a `sendall` failure wrapped as `QMPError`, and the existing closed-by-peer path re-verified in the new file — all against real local TCP sockets standing in for QEMU's QMP server, no monkeypatching of socket internals needed for the reproducing cases); added `tests/test_qmp.py` to the CI test list and the README's test-file list. ## Follow-ups discovered (not started)
- [ ] `QMPClient`'s connect timeout (20s) and per-read timeout (15s) are hardcoded with no way to override them - a genuinely slow host (e.g. a loaded CI runner, or a QMP command like `migrate` that can legitimately take longer than 15s to return) has no escape hatch short of patching the source.
- [ ] A VM that exits on its own outside of `qemu_stop` (guest kernel panic that also kills the QEMU process, or `quit` sent via the `qemu_qmp` escape hatch) stays in `qemu_list`'s registry as `exited(...)` forever, and its workdir is never cleaned up, until something calls `qemu_stop` on it explicitly. Consider reaping dead entries (and their workdirs) the next time `qemu_list` or `qemu_boot` runs.
